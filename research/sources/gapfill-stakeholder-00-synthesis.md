Research completed: 2026-09-05  
Scope: accessibility for aphasia/cognitive impairment, caregiver inclusion, and therapist-facing information needs.

## Product implications

1. **Design for impaired motor, visual, language, and cognitive function together.** Use large touch targets, high contrast, short one-idea-at-a-time copy, predictable navigation, visible status/confirmation, and multimodal alternatives (text, audio, symbols, and clinician/caregiver-supported completion).
2. **Make aphasia support a first-class mode, not a help article.** Let survivors choose language and communication preferences; offer simplified prompts, extra time, repetition, and a low-burden way to indicate “I need help.” Validate with people with aphasia and speech-language professionals.
3. **Include caregivers with explicit survivor-controlled permissions.** Support a caregiver view for agreed tasks, shared goals, appointment/context notes, and communication with the team; clearly show who can see or change each item. Avoid making caregiver proxy completion the default measure of survivor engagement.
4. **Give therapists interpretable, actionable information.** Present trends, adherence/context, patient-reported outcomes, confidence/understanding, and caregiver observations with timestamps and provenance—not raw activity counts alone. Flag uncertainty and preserve the survivor’s own report.
5. **Build escalation and coordination into the workflow.** Provide role visibility, concise handoff summaries, communication difficulty indicators, preferred language, and clear boundaries that the app is not an emergency channel.

## Research gaps to validate next

- No source above establishes the best aphasia-friendly interaction pattern for a consumer stroke-recovery product or demonstrates outcome benefit.
- Evidence is thin on cognitive impairment, fatigue, neglect, low digital literacy, multilingual use, and assistive-technology compatibility in stroke apps.
- Caregiver research and guidance support inclusion, but do not settle consent, delegated access, notification burden, or caregiver-reported outcome design.
- Therapist-facing requirements need prospective workflow research: which patient-reported outcomes are clinically actionable, alert thresholds, review frequency, and integration with existing records.
- The 2025 app audit is Android-focused and mostly checklist/heuristic based; test iOS and real users, including survivors with aphasia and motor/visual impairment.
