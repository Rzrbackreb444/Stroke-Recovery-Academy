# Digital Stroke-Recovery Evidence and Competitive Landscape

## Bottom line

Digital tools are credible as *adjuncts* to clinician-led stroke rehabilitation, particularly when they increase dose, repetition, feedback, or access to supervised care. The evidence does not justify treating a generic app, AI coach, gamification layer, or remote-monitoring dashboard as an independently proven treatment. Outcomes vary by deficit, intervention content, therapist involvement, duration, and adherence.

## Evidence of efficacy

- **Mobile apps:** A systematic review of 29 studies (11 randomized and 18 quasi-experimental) found signals for therapy apps targeting motor paresis/aphasia and for videos or reminders targeting exercise adherence. Evidence for ADLs, quality of life, prevention, mood, and other app/outcome combinations was limited or non-significant. The authors emphasize task-specific, massed, goal-oriented practice, feedback, and multisensory cueing rather than “app” status itself.
- **Mobile intervention meta-analysis:** A 2023 review included 23 controlled studies and 2,983 survivors. It evaluated medication adherence, function, cardiovascular risks, quality of life, and knowledge—not a single uniform digital therapeutic. The heterogeneity, variable professional involvement, and limited adherence measurement mean pooled results should not be read as proof that any commercial app restores function.
- **Telerehabilitation:** A multicenter randomized noninferiority trial (124 adults, 4–36 weeks post-stroke) compared therapist-supervised home telerehabilitation with in-clinic therapy for arm motor recovery. The remote model was designed to achieve comparable improvement under a structured protocol; this supports remote delivery when therapists, assessment, equipment, and safety procedures remain involved. It is not evidence that unsupervised video exercise or AI alone is equivalent.
- **Speech/cognitive tools:** Digital language and cognitive exercises are plausible and have small trials/usability studies, but broad claims of durable aphasia or cognition recovery require deficit-specific controlled outcomes. Content, dose, accessibility, and clinician adjustment matter more than the presence of speech recognition or a chatbot.
- **Gamification, monitoring, and AI coaching:** These have credible mechanisms (feedback, motivation, repetition, and visibility into home practice), but evidence is mostly early-stage, heterogeneous, or feasibility/usability-oriented. A design study of an AI/robotic coach, for example, involved interviews and a very small survivor evaluation; it demonstrates acceptability/design requirements, not efficacy. “AI-powered” is a positioning claim until validated against clinical outcomes.

## Usability and adherence (not efficacy)

Reminders, progress feedback, videos, goals, and game mechanics can support engagement, but engagement is an intermediate outcome. Studies often measure session completion, satisfaction, or short-term adherence rather than blinded functional outcomes maintained after the intervention. Survivors may have aphasia, neglect, hemiparesis, fatigue, visual impairment, cognitive impairment, or low digital confidence; onboarding, caregiver/therapist support, adjustable difficulty, accessible controls, and escalation for pain or deterioration are therefore core safety/usability requirements. Remote monitoring should report observable practice and patient-reported status with clear uncertainty, not imply that passive sensor data equals recovery.

## Competitive landscape

The following is **positioning/product-surface evidence**, not proof of efficacy:

| Product/category | Publicly described offer | Evidence interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Tactus Therapy | App suite focused on aphasia, cognition, reading, writing, and speech-language exercises; clinician-oriented customization is emphasized. | A focused tool can complement speech therapy; product pages are not a substitute for independent controlled trials of the specific app and dose. |
| Constant Therapy | Subscription cognitive, speech, and language exercise platform with adaptive task selection and progress tracking. | Adaptive practice and tracking are plausible adherence features; marketing claims should be separated from independently measured language outcomes. |
| Flint Rehab FitMi | Home exercise hardware/software and activity tracking aimed at increasing repetitions for arm, hand, and whole-body rehabilitation. | Hardware-supported repetition may address dose; verify population, comparator, therapist oversight, and durability of outcomes. |
| Saebo digital/home-rehab ecosystem | Exercise and home-rehabilitation tools, education, and equipment for upper-limb recovery. | A broad equipment ecosystem is not evidence that every listed exercise or app improves outcomes. |
| Telerehabilitation providers/clinic platforms | Video visits, prescribed home programs, remote check-ins, and sometimes device or sensor data. | Best-supported when integrated with licensed therapists and clinical assessment; “remote” alone is not a therapeutic mechanism. |

The product opportunity is therefore not to claim a novel cure. A differentiated, safer position is a survivor-first adherence and coordination layer: personalized goals, accessible daily practice, transparent progress, caregiver/therapist sharing, and escalation when symptoms or safety signals warrant human review.

## Product implications and evidence gaps

1. Define each feature’s intended outcome (practice dose, adherence, speech task performance, arm function, or quality of life) and measure it separately.
2. Label clinician-reviewed education and wellness coaching distinctly from medical treatment; do not present AI-generated guidance as diagnosis or individualized clinical advice.
3. Instrument adherence and drop-off, but report it separately from efficacy. Pre-register or prospectively evaluate clinically meaningful outcomes before making recovery claims.
4. Build accessibility, caregiver support, therapist export, and safety escalation into the core experience rather than adding gamification first.
5. For AI coaching, begin with bounded explanations, reminders, and motivational support; require human review for symptom changes, exercise progression, medication, or urgent concerns.
