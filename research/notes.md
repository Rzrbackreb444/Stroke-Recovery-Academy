## Findings Log

### Triangulated findings

1. Stroke survivors and caregivers have persistent needs across information, fatigue, cognition, communication, participation, care coordination, home support, transport, finances, and long-term planning—not only exercise.
2. Clinicians need bounded, accessible, provenance-rich information that helps with goals, handoffs, and preparation; engagement counts alone are not clinically actionable.
3. Digital tools are credible as adjuncts for practice, education, reminders, tracking, exports, and between-visit support. The evidence does not justify autonomous diagnosis, prescribing, or rehabilitation progression.
4. Accessibility for aphasia, cognition, fatigue, motor and vision impairment is a product requirement. Existing stroke apps show recurring touch-target, contrast, status, and comprehension failures.
5. Public comparables support both a focused subscription near $30/month and a lower-friction one-time $10–$30 product. General health-app willingness to pay is materially lower and sensitive to trust, affordability, and perceived fit.
6. Meaningful revenue is plausible through a hybrid of survivor products, modest continuity membership, and provider/nonprofit pilots. Payer reimbursement requires later evidence and economic proof.
7. The Academy’s survivor-led voice, STUCK product, and next-step loop are directionally right. The next release should simplify Today, strengthen consent/privacy/accessibility/reliability, and measure care-readiness rather than neurological recovery.

## Conflicts & Open Questions

## Conflicts & Open Questions

- Search guidance and survivor resources identify strong qualitative intent clusters, but exact query volume and commercial intent still require analysis of the attached Search export.
- Vendor pricing confirms what competitors ask, not conversion, retention, profitability, or clinical efficacy.
- Payer and DTx evidence sources are not stroke-specific; provider willingness to pay must be tested directly.
- Consumer subscription versus provider-sponsored distribution remains an empirical product decision.
- The first owned outcome should be care-readiness/next-step follow-through, not a claim about functional recovery.
