Here’s a deeper, more comprehensive list of stroke recovery exercises, organized from lower body to upper body, with seated and standing variations. I’ve included three levels (Beginner/Mild deficits, Moderate, Advanced) with recommended reps and sets based on evidence-based guidelines (e.g., AHA recommendations for 10–15 reps, 1–3 sets, 2–3 days/week for strength; higher volume for repetition-based neuroplasticity; shorter holds for early weight bearing to avoid fatigue or spasticity).
Critical Safety Reminders (display prominently on your platform):
•  These are general suggestions drawn from common evidence-based practices. Every stroke and recovery is unique. Consult your doctor, physical therapist (PT), or occupational therapist (OT) before starting. Stop immediately if you feel pain (beyond mild discomfort), dizziness, shortness of breath, or other unusual symptoms.
•  Focus on quality movement and controlled breathing (exhale during effort). Start slow. Progress only when movements feel safe and controlled.
•  Prioritize weight bearing where appropriate for proprioception, spasticity reduction, and neuroplasticity, but always with support and supervision initially.
•  Frequency: 2–3 sessions per day, short durations (10–20 minutes) are often better than one long session. Rest as needed between sets.
•  Use support (chair, wall, table, or caregiver) as needed. Mirror feedback helps with alignment.
Lower Body Exercises (Focus: Legs, Hips, Ankles, Feet, Balance)
Seated Variations (Ideal for early recovery, poor balance, or fatigue)
•  Ankle Pumps / Dorsiflexion (All levels – especially good for drop foot and circulation) Point toes down, then pull toes up toward shin. Beginner: 2 sets of 10–15 reps per foot, slow and controlled. Moderate: 3 sets of 15–20 reps, add pause at top (2–3 sec). Advanced: 3 sets of 20–30 reps or add light resistance band.
•  Seated Marching (Beginner–Moderate – hip flexion, core) Lift one knee toward chest (or as high as comfortable), alternate. Beginner: 2 sets of 8–12 reps per leg. Moderate: 3 sets of 12–20 reps, add arm swing. Advanced: 3 sets of 20+ reps or hold knee up 3–5 sec.
•  Seated Leg Extensions (Moderate – quad strength) Straighten one knee fully, hold, lower slowly. Beginner: 2 sets of 8–10 reps per leg. Moderate: 3 sets of 10–15 reps, hold 3–5 sec at top. Advanced: 3 sets of 15–20 reps with light ankle weight.
•  Seated Heel Slides (Moderate – knee flexion) Slide heel toward buttocks, then extend leg. Beginner: 2 sets of 8–10 reps per leg. Moderate/Advanced: 3 sets of 10–15 reps, add gentle resistance.
•  Seated Weight Shifts (All levels – early weight bearing intro) Shift weight side-to-side or forward/back while seated. Beginner: Hold 5–10 sec each direction, 2 sets of 8–10 shifts. Moderate: Hold 10–20 sec, 3 sets. Advanced: Add arm reaches or eyes closed.
Standing Variations (Moderate–Advanced – add support initially)
•  Weight Shifting (Moderate – core weight bearing for balance and spasticity) Stand with feet shoulder-width, shift weight side-to-side or forward/back. Beginner/Moderate: Hold 5–15 sec per direction, 2–3 sets of 8–12 shifts (use chair/wall). Advanced: Hold 20–30 sec, add single-leg emphasis or eyes closed.
•  Sit-to-Stand (Moderate–Advanced – functional strength) Rise from chair using legs (minimal arm help as able). Moderate: 2–3 sets of 5–10 reps (use arms for support initially). Advanced: 3 sets of 10–15 reps, slow eccentric lower.
•  Mini Squats / Supported Squats (Moderate–Advanced) Bend knees slightly while keeping back straight. Moderate: 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps (hold chair). Advanced: 3 sets of 12–15 reps, deeper range or single-leg variation.
•  Calf Raises / Heel Raises (Advanced – ankle strength) Rise onto toes, lower slowly. Moderate: 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps (both feet, hold support). Advanced: 3 sets of 12–20 reps, progress to single affected leg.
•  Step-Ups or Lateral Steps (Advanced – coordination, hip stability) Step onto low sturdy surface or step sideways. Moderate: 2–3 sets of 8–10 reps per leg (hold rail). Advanced: 3 sets of 10–15 reps, increase height/speed.
Drop Foot / Ankle Specific (Integrate across levels)
•  Seated: Ankle alphabet or resisted dorsiflexion with band (2–3 sets of 10–20).
•  Standing: Toe taps or heel walks (if safe; 2–3 sets of 10–15).
Upper Body Exercises (Focus: Shoulders, Arms, Hands, Elbows, Wrists)
Seated Variations (Safer for balance issues)
•  Shoulder Shrugs / Rolls (Beginner – reduce tightness) Shrug shoulders up, then roll forward/back. Beginner: 2 sets of 10–15 reps. Moderate/Advanced: 3 sets of 15–20 reps, add light weights.
•  Arm Raises (Front or Side) (Beginner–Moderate) Lift affected arm forward/sideways (assist with unaffected hand if needed). Beginner: 2 sets of 8–10 reps, hold 3 sec. Moderate: 3 sets of 10–15 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 15–20 reps with light weight.
•  Elbow Flexion/Extension (Beginner–Moderate) Bend and straighten elbow slowly. Beginner: 2 sets of 8–12 reps. Moderate/Advanced: 3 sets of 12–20 reps, add towel roll or light weight.
•  Wrist Flexion/Extension (Moderate) Bend wrist up/down (use unaffected hand for gentle assist/stretch). Moderate: 2–3 sets of 10–15 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 15–20 reps with resistance.
•  Finger Opens / Grip Squeeze (All levels – spasticity & hand function) Open hand wide, then squeeze soft ball or towel. Beginner: 2 sets of 10–15 reps. Moderate: 3 sets of 15–20 reps, add hold. Advanced: 3 sets of 20+ reps or use therapy putty.
•  Table Top Weight Bearing (Moderate – key upper body weight bearing) Place affected arm on table (elbow straight if possible), lean forward to bear weight through shoulder/elbow/wrist. Beginner/Moderate: Hold 10–30 sec, 2–3 sets (build tolerance). Advanced: Hold 45–60 sec or add reaching while bearing weight.
Standing Variations (Moderate–Advanced)
•  Wall Lean / Modified Push (Moderate–Advanced – shoulder stability & weight bearing) Hands on wall at shoulder height, lean in gently. Moderate: Hold 10–30 sec or 2–3 sets of 8–12 small pushes. Advanced: 3 sets of 10–15 modified push-ups.
•  Overhead Reach (Moderate–Advanced) Reach arms overhead (assist if needed). Moderate: 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 12–20 reps with light weight.
•  Bilateral Arm Swings (Advanced – coordination) Swing arms as if walking. Advanced: 3 sets of 20–30 swings.
Hand/Fine Motor (Integrate across levels)
•  Towel scrunches, finger taps, picking up objects, or buttoning practice once ready.
General Guidelines for All Exercises & Levels
•  Beginner/Mild: Focus on range of motion and gentle activation. Shorter holds, fewer reps (emphasize quality and safety). 1–2 sessions/day.
•  Moderate: Add holds, controlled speed, and light resistance. Build endurance.
•  Advanced: Increase reps/sets, add resistance (bands/weights), complexity (balance challenges, dual tasks), or speed.
•  Progression Rule: When an exercise feels easy for 2–3 sessions, increase reps/sets/hold time or reduce support. Always prioritize safety and form.
•  Weight Bearing Emphasis: Start early with supported positions (table leans, wall pushes, weight shifts) — they provide sensory input that aids neuroplasticity and reduces spasticity. Build duration gradually (10 sec → 60+ sec).
•  Frequency: Aim for consistency over intensity. Short daily sessions often yield better results than sporadic long ones.
•  Pair with S.A.S.C.H.: Use these exercises within the formula — Seize your current level, Absorb feedback, Swing through the movement, Combine with other exercises, Hammer daily with consistency.
