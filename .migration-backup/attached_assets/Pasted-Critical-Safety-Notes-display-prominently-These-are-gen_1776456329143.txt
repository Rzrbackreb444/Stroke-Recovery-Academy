Critical Safety Notes (display prominently):
•  These are general suggestions. Every stroke and recovery is unique. Consult your doctor, physical therapist (PT), or occupational therapist (OT) before starting. Stop if you feel sharp pain, dizziness, or unusual symptoms.
•  Focus on quality and control. Breathe normally (exhale during effort). Start with fewer reps/sets and build slowly.
•  Spasticity can worsen with fatigue or overstretching — go slow and gentle.
•  Weight bearing helps reduce spasticity when done safely with support.
Spasticity Management (Before or Between Exercises)
Spasticity (muscle tightness/stiffness) is common after stroke. The goal is to reduce it through gentle, consistent movement rather than forcing stretches.
General Spasticity Tips (All Levels):
•  Move slowly and breathe deeply.
•  Use weight bearing positions early (they provide sensory input that can calm overactive muscles).
•  Alternate movement with short rests.
•  Avoid sudden or forceful stretches.
Simple Spasticity-Reduction Techniques:
•  Gentle Rocking (Seated or Standing with support): Rock side-to-side or forward/back for 30–60 seconds. Helps desensitize muscles.
•  Weight Bearing Holds: See lower and upper body sections below — holding safe weight-bearing positions (e.g., arm on table or standing weight shifts) for 10–30+ seconds can reduce tone.
•  Rhythmic Movement: Slow, repetitive motions (e.g., ankle pumps or arm swings) in a steady rhythm often calm spasticity better than static holds.
•  Positioning: Keep affected limbs in neutral or stretched positions when resting (e.g., hand open with towel roll, ankle in slight dorsiflexion).
Recommended Approach: Do 1–2 spasticity techniques before starting exercises, and again if tightness increases during the session.
Lower Body Exercises (Legs, Hips, Ankles, Feet)
Seated Variations
•  Ankle Pumps / Dorsiflexion (Great for drop foot and circulation) Point toes down, then pull toes up toward shin. Beginner: 2 sets of 10–15 reps per foot. Moderate: 3 sets of 15–20 reps, pause 2–3 sec at top. Advanced: 3 sets of 20–30 reps or add light band.
•  Seated Marching (Hip flexion, core) Lift one knee toward chest, alternate. Beginner: 2 sets of 8–12 reps per leg. Moderate: 3 sets of 12–20 reps, add arm swing. Advanced: 3 sets of 20+ reps or hold knee up 3–5 sec.
•  Seated Leg Extensions (Quad strength) Straighten one knee fully, hold, lower slowly. Beginner: 2 sets of 8–10 reps per leg. Moderate: 3 sets of 10–15 reps, hold 3–5 sec. Advanced: 3 sets of 15–20 reps with light ankle weight.
•  Seated Heel Slides (Knee flexion) Slide heel toward buttocks, then extend. Beginner: 2 sets of 8–10 reps per leg. Moderate/Advanced: 3 sets of 10–15 reps.
•  Seated Weight Shifts (Early weight bearing) Shift weight side-to-side or forward/back while seated. Beginner: Hold 5–10 sec, 2 sets of 8–10 shifts. Moderate: Hold 10–20 sec, 3 sets. Advanced: Add arm reaches.
Standing Variations (Use support initially)
•  Weight Shifting (Balance and spasticity reduction) Shift weight side-to-side or forward/back. Moderate: Hold 5–15 sec, 2–3 sets of 8–12 shifts. Advanced: Hold 20–30 sec or single-leg emphasis.
•  Sit-to-Stand (Functional leg strength) Rise from chair using legs. Moderate: 2–3 sets of 5–10 reps (use arms initially). Advanced: 3 sets of 10–15 reps, slow lower.
•  Mini Squats (Quad and glute strength) Bend knees slightly. Moderate: 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps (hold chair). Advanced: 3 sets of 12–15 reps.
•  Calf Raises (Ankle strength) Rise onto toes. Moderate: 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps (both feet). Advanced: 3 sets of 12–20 reps, progress to single leg.
•  Step-Ups or Lateral Steps (Coordination) Moderate: 2–3 sets of 8–10 reps per leg (hold rail). Advanced: 3 sets of 10–15 reps.
Drop Foot Specific: Ankle alphabet or resisted dorsiflexion with band (2–3 sets of 10–20).
Upper Body Exercises
Seated Variations
•  Shoulder Shrugs/Rolls (Reduce tightness) Beginner: 2 sets of 10–15 reps. Moderate/Advanced: 3 sets of 15–20 reps.
•  Arm Raises (Front/Side) Beginner: 2 sets of 8–10 reps, hold 3 sec. Moderate: 3 sets of 10–15 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 15–20 reps with light weight.
•  Elbow Flexion/Extension Beginner: 2 sets of 8–12 reps. Moderate/Advanced: 3 sets of 12–20 reps.
•  Wrist Flexion/Extension Moderate: 2–3 sets of 10–15 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 15–20 reps with resistance.
•  Finger Opens / Grip Squeeze (Hand function) Beginner: 2 sets of 10–15 reps. Moderate: 3 sets of 15–20 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 20+ reps or therapy putty.
•  Table Top Weight Bearing (Key for shoulder stability and spasticity) Lean into affected arm on table (elbow straight if possible). Beginner/Moderate: Hold 10–30 sec, 2–3 sets. Advanced: Hold 45–60 sec or add reaching.
Standing Variations
•  Wall Lean / Modified Push (Shoulder weight bearing) Moderate: Hold 10–30 sec or 2–3 sets of 8–12 small pushes. Advanced: 3 sets of 10–15 modified push-ups.
•  Overhead Reach Moderate: 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps. Advanced: 3 sets of 12–20 reps.
•  Bilateral Arm Swings (Coordination) Advanced: 3 sets of 20–30 swings.
Stretching (After Movement or Spasticity Work)
Stretching helps maintain range of motion and reduce spasticity. Hold gently — never force. Breathe deeply. 20–60 seconds per stretch, 2–3 times.
Lower Body Stretches
•  Calf Stretch (Seated or Standing): Sit with leg extended, loop towel around foot and gently pull toes toward you. Or stand facing wall, one foot back, heel down.
•  Hamstring Stretch (Seated): Sit tall, extend one leg, hinge forward from hips until gentle stretch behind knee.
•  Hip Flexor Stretch (Seated or Standing): Sit at edge of chair, extend one leg back, gently press hips forward (or lunge position with support).
•  Inner Thigh Stretch: Sit with soles of feet together, gently press knees down.
Upper Body Stretches
•  Shoulder Stretch: Bring affected arm across body with unaffected hand, gentle pull.
•  Chest Opener: Clasp hands behind back (or use towel), lift arms slightly.
•  Wrist/Finger Stretch: Gently extend fingers and wrist backward with unaffected hand.
•  Neck Stretch: Tilt head side to side or forward/back gently.
Spasticity-Specific Stretching Tip: Perform after light movement or weight bearing. Use slow, sustained holds rather than bouncing. Combine with deep breathing to calm the nervous system.
How to Use This List on Your Platform
•  Organize as progressive modules: “Lower Body Builder – Beginner”, etc.
•  Encourage users to pair exercises with S.A.S.C.H.: Seize current level, Absorb feedback, Swing through the movement, Combine with stretching, Hammer daily.
•  Suggest 10–20 minute sessions, 2–3 times daily.
•  Add progress tracking: “How did spasticity feel today? Any new flickers?”
This gives survivors a safe, progressive path from spasticity management through movement and stretching, with realistic reps/sets that respect different deficits and energy levels.
