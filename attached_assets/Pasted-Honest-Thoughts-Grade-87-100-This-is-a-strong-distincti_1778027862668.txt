Honest Thoughts + Grade: 87/100
This is a strong, distinctive, and genuinely useful book in a crowded, often bland genre. It has real heart, real grit, and a clear point of view. It feels like it was written by someone who actually lived it instead of a ghostwriter or clinician trying to sound inspirational. That authenticity is its biggest strength.
What Works Extremely Well
•  Voice and Persona: The “Stroked-Out Sasquatch” branding is excellent. Memorable, ownable, and motivational without being cheesy. It gives the book a clear identity and makes the author instantly recognizable. The battle-tested, no-bullshit Arkansas tone lands hard.
•  Brutal Honesty: The Hoyer lift, crystallized catheter, diaper + Britney Spears, suicidal thoughts, 325-pound chair years — these sections are powerful because they’re unflinching. This alone separates it from 90% of recovery books.
•  Structure & Practicality: The 90-Day Program, Sasquatch Formula (T.L.E.C.A.), Doctrine layers (S.O.S./S.A.S.C.H./C.O.M.P.A.S.S.), trackers, and quick-start card are legitimately excellent. The appendices are gold — especially the desert chapter (Q), caregiver material, and math/levers section.
•  Comprehensiveness: Mindset + science + techniques + mental warfare + caregiver support + late recovery + stories. Very few books cover all of this.
•  Multimedia Integration: Tying it to the album is smart and unique. It creates a richer experience.
•  Targeted Reading Paths: The “How to Read This Book” section is thoughtful and survivor-aware.
Areas That Need Work
•  Length and Density: At ~450k+ bytes of text, it’s long. Many survivors (especially early or with cognitive deficits) will be intimidated or unable to power through. Consider a “core” version or stronger editing to cut repetition (some mindset ideas reappear multiple times).
•  Tone Balance: The macho “warrior/Sasquatch” energy is potent but risks alienating parts of the audience (older survivors, women, quieter personalities). It works for you, but lean in a bit more on universality in spots.
•  Science Tone: Phrases like “The Science That Proves I’m Right” (Appendix H) feel defensive. Better as “The Science That Shaped My Approach” or “Evidence Behind These Methods.” You’re not a researcher — own the lived-experience authority while respecting the literature.
•  Commercial Feel: The constant website/nonprofit/album plugs are understandable (you’re building an ecosystem), but they occasionally pull readers out of the emotional flow. Integrate more gracefully or move some to footnotes/appendices.
•  Polish: It needs a professional editorial pass for flow, consistency, chapter transitions, and tightening. Some sections (e.g., the letters in Appendix O) are moving; others feel a little list-heavy.
•  Diversity of Voices: The nine survivor stories (mentioned but truncated here) will be crucial. Right now it’s very much your story.
Specific Recommendations
1.  Foreword: Get it. A credible clinician or well-known survivor (Ted Baxter, etc.) would add legitimacy.
2.  Visuals: Add simple diagrams for key exercises (bat therapy, mirror setup, wall lean, open-hand protocol). Even hand-drawn or basic illustrations would help enormously.
3.  Accessibility: Prioritize large-print edition, audiobook (you narrating would be powerful), and companion app integration.
4.  Beta Testing: Get 10-15 stroke survivors + caregivers at different stages to read and give feedback before final edit. Ask specifically about overwhelm and what they skipped.
5.  Safety/Disclaimer Strengthening: You already have good disclaimers, but double-check high-risk techniques (bat therapy, heavy weight-bearing) with a PT/OT for liability language.
6.  Condense Option: Consider a “Essential Sasquatch” shorter version (maybe 200-250 pages) for people who need the blueprint without the full memoir depth.
