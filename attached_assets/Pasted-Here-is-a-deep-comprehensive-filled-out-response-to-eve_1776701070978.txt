CATEGORY 1 — The Stroke Itself (Getting the Facts Exact)
Confirmed details: On December 3, 2018, I suffered a massive hemorrhagic stroke caused by a blood clot on the right side of my head (right hemisphere damage), which affected the left side of my body — classic crossed symptoms. I was standing and talking to a friend when I started slurring. My left side dropped. It was a perfect BE FAST scenario: face drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty, time to call 911. Lisa yelled, “You’re having a stroke!” I slurred back, “Nooo Iiiiaaan noooot!” and tried to stand but collapsed as my left side went completely limp.
I was rushed to the hospital in Fort Smith. They gave my family the choice: fly me by helicopter to Little Rock for surgery or do it locally ASAP. My parents wanted Little Rock, but Lisa (pregnant at the time) made the call for immediate surgery there because my brain was still swelling badly. That decision saved my life.
They performed an emergency craniotomy. The neurosurgeon had to remove the clot but sucked it up and lost it in the vacuum during the procedure, so they couldn’t culture it to see exactly what caused the bleed. He pounded 50 staples into my skull to close it. He joked that he almost didn’t make it through my skull because I was “so damn thick-headed.” I was hopped up on pain meds and he said I shouldn’t feel anything — boy was he wrong. It felt like daggers being driven into my skull. Hell on earth.
ICU stay: About a month. I lost around 50 pounds from being on tube feedings and pureed foods. Swallowing was destroyed (dysphagia). They crushed meds in applesauce; it took me an hour to get it down. One night I woke up choking to death on the feeding tube. Lisa ripped it out like a warrior — blood everywhere, chaos, nurses rushing in. She truly saved my life that night. She was pregnant, skipped meals, only left once a week or so to shower, and stayed by my side the whole time even though we weren’t romantic anymore (I had been trying to divorce her before the stroke and was skeptical when she said she was pregnant — I made her prove it with a doctor visit because I thought she was lying to keep me around. I was an asshole about it, partly because I hated how passive she seemed compared to my strong mom).
Morphine hallucinations: Whenever Lisa wasn’t there, the morphine made me paranoid. I thought the hospital staff was trying to kill me. Chainsaw blades buzzing in my skull. Pure terror.
Deficits coming out: Complete left-side paralysis (arm hung heavy, leg dead weight), severe speech issues (could barely talk, words crumbled), swallowing problems (dangerous, tube-dependent at first), balance gone, fine motor zero, cognitive fog, and massive identity collapse. I gave a thumbs-up with my right hand to family as they wheeled me to the OR.
Prognosis words: A well-meaning medical professional said, “You’ll probably never walk normally again.” That “never” became my forge.
Remaining 10% I’m still working on today (2026): Fine motor control in my left hand (buttoning shirts, precise grip still slower), ankle stability and drop foot remnants (occasional fatigue or slight drag when tired), and overall endurance/fatigue management. I’m at 90%+ and still rising — memory processing has even doubled in recent breakthroughs — but I keep swinging because construction never stops. All strokes differ; this is my blueprint, not a guarantee.
Disclaimer everywhere: This is lived experience, not medical advice. Consult your care team. Results vary.

CATEGORY 4 — The Three Systems (Filling in the Gaps)
S.A.S.C.H.: My daily operating system. Seize the day (ownership), Absorb the lesson, Swing the work (even when it hurts), Combine elements (experiment), Hammer it home with reflection. This is the everyday hammer for construction.
C.O.M.P.A.S.S. (Advanced navigation):
•  Control what you can (rituals, small wins, environment).
•  Own your pace (no 30-day miracles; tortoise wins).
•  Mutate/adapt when stuck (change angles, tools, goals).
•  Patience with the timeline, body, and people (nutrition fuels the wait).
•  Anger as fuel (channel it clean — burn it in reps, not rage).
•  Small wins are sacred (toe twitch, buttoning a shirt, one extra second of balance).
•  Stack wins on wins and failures into successes (every miss = data).
S.O.S. (Panic button / beginner reset): Survive (breathe, anchor why), Organize (structure the day), Strive (push one rep). Use S.O.S. in early chaos or dark days; switch to full S.A.S.C.H. as momentum builds. A complete S.O.S. day: Morning gratitude + basic self-care, midday movement, evening reflection/stack.
All three stack cleanly: S.O.S. for survival mode, S.A.S.C.H. for daily construction, C.O.M.P.A.S.S. for advanced mastery. “Recovery is construction” ties them together.
CATEGORY 5 — The Methodology in Practice
Non-negotiable core exercises (even on worst days): Mirror therapy (discovered in rehab — trick the brain by watching the good side), baseball bat therapy (Michael’s $5 bat for balance/standing — doubled my time), weight-bearing (standing shifts, negative pressure), think-flinch-existence progression, chopping wood (visualize or do one rep). Daily: teeth brushing/grooming/showering with adaptations, stretching, persona push (“Sasquatch doesn’t skip”).
What failed: Over-relying on passive help, waiting for motivation, robotic glove (sometimes made things worse by discouraging active effort).
Most counterintuitive: Mental rehearsal works almost as well as physical early on (motor imagery activates same circuits). Creativity (baseball bat, balloons) beats strict protocols. Failures are data, not defeat. “Never” is just “not yet.”
CATEGORY 6 — The 66-Day Challenge (The Core Program)
Why 66 days: Research (Lally et al.) shows it takes an average of 66 days for a new behavior to become automatic (range 18–254 days). It’s the start of a lifelong routine, not a guarantee — all strokes differ, so consistency with S.A.S.C.H. builds momentum. Nothing is promised; the work must still be done, but I’m living proof it’s possible.
Day 1 example: Wake with gratitude (“This is my second chance — what do I still have?”). Basic self-care: teeth brushing/grooming/showering (seated options, adaptive tools). Light stretching + one persona push (“Sasquatch swings anyway”). Log 1 win and 1 failure reframed as data. Evening reflection: “What did I build today?”
Three phases:
•  Survival (Days 1–22): Basics — hygiene, simple movement, survive the day, anchor purpose (son as spark), build S.O.S. habits.
•  Breakthrough (Days 23–44): Layer consistency, add S.A.S.C.H. daily, increase reps, use persona to push through plateaus, stack small wins.
•  Optimization (Days 45–66): Full routines (stretching/exercise progressions, grooming independence, gratitude as default), stack failures into successes, see the “new life” as blessing, prepare for lifelong construction.
Completion & results: Not everyone makes it — life happens, motivation dips, overwhelm hits. Typical quit points: around day 10–14 (when novelty wears off) or day 30–40 (plateau). Those who finish report the #1 result: a solid daily routine that feels automatic, reduced depression (“I’m building again”), more independence in self-care, and mindset shift from “what I lost” to “what I’m constructing.” Many say it gave them hope and structure when everything felt chaotic. This is based on my journey + community feedback; we’re testing/refining it live at the Academy.
