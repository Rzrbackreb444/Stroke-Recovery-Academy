name: Survivor-first trust boundary
description: Product language and structured data must distinguish Nick's lived experience from clinical claims or universal recovery promises.
---

Nick's recovery story can be presented as attributed lived expertise: knowledge absorbed from therapists, books, and research, then applied, repeated, documented, and adapted through his own recovery. Stroke Recovery Academy should describe itself as a lived-recovery system and education platform—not medical treatment or a medical-grade platform. Avoid universal rates, outcome guarantees, coercive “push through” language, unsupported clinical credentials, and claims that ordinary props outperform clinical equipment.

**Why:** Survivors and caregivers need honest expectations, and the product's safety direction depends on separating a founder testimonial from advice that could be mistaken for individualized medical guidance.

**How to apply:** Lead with Nick's expert-by-experience process—learn, apply, repeat, document, adapt, and stack small goals—while keeping personal percentages and timelines explicitly attributed to him. Pair activity guidance with pacing, pause/skip, clinician/support, and emergency framing where relevant; use plain-language privacy and AI-boundary disclosures; audit new recovery, pricing, SEO, book, and Coach copy for contradictions before shipping.
