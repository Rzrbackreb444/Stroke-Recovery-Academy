## Reddit — value posts

### 1. "What I wish someone told me in the first 90 days"

**Title:** Seven years out. Here's what I'd tell my 90-days-post-stroke self.

I had my stroke at 36. Complete left-side paralysis — couldn't lift the arm,
couldn't trust the leg. Today I'm somewhere around ninety percent back. Not
because of one miracle. Because of a thousand boring reps nobody claps for.

Three things I'd hand my earlier self:

- Plateaus are a lie your calendar tells you. Progress goes quiet, not gone.
- Fatigue isn't weakness — it's your brain rewiring on a budget. Rest is reps.
- The gains come from what you do on the days you don't feel like it.

Whatever you're staring down right now — the arm that won't answer, the word
that won't come — it's not the final draft. Keep the reps coming.

*(I write about this stuff — happy to point you to a longer breakdown if it'd
help, just ask. And if a doctor or PT tells you different for your situation,
listen to them over a stranger on the internet.)*

### 2. Answer-style post — equipment question

**Title:** Cheap gear that actually moved the needle for me (and stuff that didn't)

People ask me what to buy. Honest answer: most of it you don't need, and the two
things that helped most were dirt cheap.

What earned its place:
- A set of resistance bands. Scalable, forgiving, works the affected side without
  a spotter.
- A grip strengthener I could squeeze on the couch. Reps add up when the tool's
  in reach.

What I wasted money on: gadgets that promised to "retrain" the brain for me. The
brain retrains from *your* effort, not a device's.

Before you spend a dollar, ask your PT or OT what fits your stage — the right
tool at the wrong stage is just clutter. Full disclosure: I run a
stroke-recovery site and some of my gear links are affiliate (small cut to me,
same price to you). Happy to share the rundown if it's useful.

## Quick comment replies

Use these when someone posts a struggle. Help lands in the comment; the link is
optional and only if it truly fits.

- **On a plateau:** "Seven years out here — what looks like a plateau is usually
  your brain consolidating before the next jump. Keep the reps steady. Talk to
  your PT about switching up the drill to break the stall."
- **On fatigue:** "Post-stroke fatigue is real and it's not you being lazy — it's
  rewiring on a budget. Pace, don't push through. Worth flagging to your doctor
  if it's sudden or getting worse."
- **On 'will I ever get back to normal':** "You may not get the old normal. You
  can build a new one that's more yours than you'd believe right now. I'm proof,
  and I'm not special — just stubborn about the reps."
